INTERPRETATION:
Pathogenic sequence variants were detected in the WT1, FLT3, and BLM genes.
The WT1 gene (11p13) encodes a zinc-finger-type transcription factor involved in cellular development
and differentiation. Mutations in the WT1 gene are found in 7–13% of AML cases and have been
associated with an adverse prognosis in some reported series. This variant had been previously detected
(April 8, 2025) in a bone marrow sample obtained prior to hematopoietic stem cell transplantation.
The FLT3 gene (13q12.2) encodes a transmembrane receptor tyrosine kinase that participates in the
differentiation, proliferation, and survival of hematopoietic precursor cells. The detected variant affects the
juxtamembrane domain of the protein and corresponds to a 39-bp internal tandem duplication (ITD). The
presence of FLT3-ITD has been reported in 32–39% of patients with acute myeloid leukemia (AML) and in

up to 2% of cases of myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS). In AML patients, FLT3 gene mutations represent a
therapeutic target.
The presence of FLT3-ITD in AML patients is associated with intermediate risk (2022 ELN AML
Recommendations).
The BLM gene (15q26.1) encodes the BLM protein, a member of the RecQ DNA helicase family. It
functions as a genomic stabilizer and is involved in the regulation of DNA replication and recombination, as
well as in the homologous and non-homologous double-strand break repair pathways. Somatic BLM
mutations have been reported in various types of neoplasms, with a low frequency in AML (1–2%) and a
prognostic significance that is currently uncertain.
Based on current knowledge, no pathogenic variants have been detected in the remaining analyzed
genes, nor has the presence of fusion genes been identified.
Correlation of these results with the clinical presentation and other complementary studies is suggested.

Methodology:
 1. Extraction and purification of genomic DNA from the submitted sample.
2. DNA library preparation by PCR, using the Community Myeloid v2 solution (MYS2, SOPHiA).
3. Hybridization with probes (MYS2 SOPHiA) targeting the regions of interest and capture thereof.
4. Clonal amplification and massive parallel sequencing of the selected regions on the NextSeq 2000
platform (Illumina).
5. Bioinformatic analysis of the obtained DNA sequence and of the fusion genes by comparison with
the reference nucleotide sequence (GRCh38).
DNA sequence variants with a variant allele frequency (VAF) > 2%, with at least 25× coverage of the
alternative allele and a minimum vertical coverage of 300×, are reported.
6. Variants are filtered using the Sophia DDM software.
7. The clinical significance of variants is determined according to the updated ACMG guidelines (PMID:
25741868), ESMO and ASCO criteria, the information available in the gnomAD, dbSNP, LOVD, and/or
ClinVar databases, gene-specific databases, and the literature available at the time of the study. The
interpretation of detected variants is based on the knowledge available at the date of the study.
Study limitations:
There are certain types of alterations that cannot be identified by this test:
 — Relevant variants in genes or regions not included in this panel.
— Variants below the established sensitivity threshold (10 reads).
Note:
 — The result of this study must always be interpreted in the context of clinical and family data.
Variants described in population databases and classified as SNPs (Single Nucleotide Polymorphisms) or
polymorphisms (MAF — minor allele frequency — ≥ 0.01 and/or included in dbSNP, ExAC, or 1000
Genomes) are not included in this report, nor are benign or likely benign variants, as, based on current

knowledge, they have no clinical impact. However, such information may be available upon medical
request.
